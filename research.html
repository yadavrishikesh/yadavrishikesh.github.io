Research

Our research group develops statistical methods for rare, high-impact events — floods, extreme rainfall, landslides, earthquakes, and wildfires — that are scarce in data yet matter most for risk. Our work builds on extreme value theory, with a focus on sub-asymptotic and multivariate models that capture how extremes cluster in space and time, and on regression models for extremes driven by covariates.

A second strand of our research is scalable spatio-temporal modeling: Bayesian hierarchical models, SPDE-based sparse representations, and dynamic generalized linear models for large environmental and urban datasets, from Himalayan monsoon rainfall to city-wide bicycle counts.

We also work at the interface of statistics and machine learning — Bayesian neural networks for uncertainty quantification, physics-informed neural networks, and generative models for climate downscaling — always with efficient Bayesian computation (MCMC) and open-source software at the core.

Packages and Tools

software
PKG·02 · NEW

sparseDGLM

R package

Sparse spatiotemporal dynamic generalized linear models for bicycle count data, using the SPDE approach for high-dimensional spatial fields. MCMC-based inference for spatial interpolation, forecasting, and annual average bicycle counts; also fits dense Matérn and Poisson GLM counterparts. Accompanies Yadav et al. (2026, Transportation Research Part B).

PKG·01

JM.landslide

R package

Joint modelling of landslide counts and sizes using a sub-asymptotic landslide size distribution. Accompanies Yadav et al. (2023, JRSSC) and the CRC Press book chapter on statistics of extremes for natural hazards.
